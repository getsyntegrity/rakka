## 1. Contract and scope

Approved by the Owner: a deadline expired or equal to the injected clock's Now returns `ErrAskTimeout` without calling `build` and without sending; `errors.Is(err, context.DeadlineExceeded)` holds; reply and timeout compete for one winner and the losing reply is dead-lettered; a context without a deadline keeps today's behavior (no timer); stopping the timer and a concurrent expiration must not alter a result already won (T8, added by the Owner). The mandatory deadline, cancellation and AK10 are #165; termination is #166. One new exported identifier: `actor.ErrAskTimeout` (ADR 0015 D3 lists the name as `[Proposal]`; the Owner accepted it on the condition above).

## 2. Deviation DEV-1 from RULEBOOK section 1.3, authorized by the Owner

RULEBOOK 1.3 approved computing `rem := deadline.Sub(clock.Now())` once, before `build`, and arming the timer after `build` with that value. That lets the timer fire up to the duration of `build` after the context's deadline, and lets a request be sent after an expired deadline. This conflicts with ADR 0015 D9 (timer duration = deadline minus Now at arming) and D5 step 2 (an expired deadline prevents the send). The Owner ordered it corrected inside this slice. `ask` keeps the approved pre-build check unchanged and now re-reads the injected clock after `build`: if time remains it arms the timer for exactly the time left, before the send; if none remains it sends nothing, arms no timer and settles the endpoint through the same single CAS (`expire`), returning the CAS winner's outcome (`ErrAskTimeout`, or a reply the endpoint received during `build`; a reply that loses is dead-lettered). T9 (build advances the fake clock), T10 (arming precedes the send, through the target cell's `told` counter) and T8c (a stub timer that records `Stop` and reports false) were added beyond RULEBOOK section 5 to pin this. The RULEBOOK text was not edited.

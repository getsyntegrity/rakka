## Exact original text replaced (`docs/adr/0010-actor-api.md`, whole bullet "Handles", line 170)

> - **Handles.** A handle of this System whose actor has terminated is a normal subject. A valid reference of this System that has no actor behind it is treated as an actor that is already dead: `Terminated` is delivered (Pekko answers a watch of a dead reference with `existenceConfirmed = false`; goPekko's `Terminated` carries no such flag). A nil handle, a handle of another System, a zero ID or a `Handle` that is not an actor reference is rejected with `ErrInvalidHandle` and is never confused with a dead actor.

## Exact proposed text (replaces the whole bullet)

> - **Handles.** `Watch`, `WatchWith` and `Unwatch` accept a native handle of this System or of another System of the same process. Three operations are distinguished, and none of the first two retains anything. (1) Obtaining the owning System from a native handle: it reads the System that the handle already holds, whether that System is running or already terminated; it consults no registry, creates no reference that the handle does not already hold and registers nothing, so it adds no retention. (2) Asking that owning System whether the actor is still active: a read-only lookup by ID in the registry of that System, made under that System's own lock, which stores nothing and adds no retention; an ID that the System does not hold means "no active actor", never "invalid handle". (3) Treating a valid handle of a retired actor as dead: `Watch` and `WatchWith` deliver `Terminated` (or the `WatchWith` message), and `Unwatch` is silent, with no notification and no error. This third operation stores no more than any watch stores: the registration, which for a subject of another System also holds the owning System until the registration ends, and the queued-termination entry until the `Terminated` is taken; both belong to the watcher and are removed when the notification is processed and taken. The owning System of a handle is resolvable if and only if the handle is non-nil, is not a typed nil, has a non-empty `ID()`, is a handle issued by a System of this package (a `Ref`, or a handle returned by `Child`, `Children`, `Parent` or `ChildFailure.Child`), and reports a non-nil owning System. The owning System is the System that issued the handle, whether it is running or already terminated. A handle whose actor has terminated is a normal subject. A valid handle that has no active actor behind it is, for `Watch` and `WatchWith`, treated as an actor that is already dead (Pekko answers a watch of a dead reference with `existenceConfirmed = false`; goPekko's `Terminated` carries no such flag). A nil handle, a zero ID, a `Handle` that is not a native handle of a System of this package, a handle whose owning System cannot be resolved, and the reply endpoint of an Ask (not an actor reference, ADR 0015 D8) are rejected with `ErrInvalidHandle` and are never confused with a dead actor. No exported API and no registry of Systems is added.

## Exact proposed new bullet, after "Handles"

> - **Subjects owned by another local System.** The rules of this section for the identity of a registration, the discard of stale notifications and the order on termination apply unchanged to a subject owned by another System of the same process. A watcher that stops is removed from the registrations of the actors it watched. For a subject of the same System this is done before `acceptStop` returns, as today. For a subject of another System the removal is handed to the executor of the subject's System as the last step of accepting the stop, after the watcher's own activation has been handed over to its own executor when one was needed. When the stop is accepted the removal has been handed off, not completed, and it may be issued after the watcher has finished. The removal does not wait for the subject to terminate: it progresses provided the executor of the subject's System runs the work it is given (assumptions E1 and E4 of the design document `cross-system-watch-design.md`, section 3.1: the executor eventually runs every function it is given, and the call to `Execute` happens before that function starts); the termination of the subject is another path that clears the same link. What is retained, by whom and for how long: a registration of a subject of another System keeps the owning System reference until the registration ends, that is, until `Unwatch`, the processing of the subject's death, or the stop of the watcher; the subject's entry for the watcher keeps the watcher's cell, and through it the watcher's System, until the first of the removal handed to the executor of the subject's System, the recheck made by `Watch` after it linked, `Unwatch`, and the termination of the subject. Between the acceptance of the stop and that first event the subject may still try to notify the watcher; a notification that a subject sends to a stopping or finished watcher is discarded (Pekko `DeathWatch.scala:109`). This covers the termination of the subject, the termination of the watcher and the shutdown of either System. `Done`, `Ask` and `Stop` do not change. Remoting and cluster are not part of this decision. This extends the acceptance of 2026-10-10; it does not replace it.

## Why, and what was verified

- Necessary: the accepted sentence makes the three calls stop the watcher with `ErrInvalidHandle` for any handle of another System (`pekko-actor/actor/watch.go:119-120`); option (b) cannot be implemented without replacing it.
- Pekko: `watch`/`watchWith` accept any `InternalActorRef` (`upstream/pekko/actor/src/main/scala/org/apache/pekko/actor/dungeon/DeathWatch.scala:35-65`); a watch of a dead or empty ref gets `existenceConfirmed = false` and `Unwatch` of one is ignored (`upstream/pekko/actor/src/main/scala/org/apache/pekko/actor/ActorRef.scala:694-699, 745-751`); a terminating watcher sends no `Terminated` (`DeathWatch.scala:109`); release by `Unwatch` is asynchronous (`DeathWatch.scala:165-180`). No Pekko spec that watches across Systems was found (design, section 1).
- What operation (3) stores, from the code: `register` stores the registration (`pekko-actor/actor/watch.go:217`); when the record is absent it calls `notifyTerminated` at once (`watch.go:179-181`), which queues a pending item; processing it deletes the registration (`watch.go:334`) and records the queued-termination entry (`watch.go:353`) until the `Terminated` is taken. `Unwatch` deletes the registration and the queued entry (`watch.go:234, 238`) and stores nothing. The owning System reference on a registration of a foreign subject is the design's new field (H1 in the design, section 3.1); it is not in the code today.
- Handle producers named in the sentence, opened: `Child` and `Children` return `rec.identity()` (`pekko-actor/actor/hierarchy.go:418, 422`, `359-378`), an `idHandle` with its System (`cell.go:93`, `hierarchy.go:68-82`); `Parent` returns the parent's `identity()` or the `/user` guardian handle (`hierarchy.go:409-415, 24-28`, `guardian.go:173`); `ChildFailure.Child` is `rec.identity()` or `idHandle{sys: c.sys}` (`supervision_failure.go:251-256`); a `Ref` is `*ref[T]` (`ref.go:28`). The Ask endpoint has a `sys` field (`ask_endpoint.go:45`) but defines no `owner()` method, so it stays rejected.
- No new API or registry: the handle already carries the System (`ref.go:28`, `hierarchy.go:82`) and nothing is registered, so there is no lifecycle to manage; a stale handle keeps its System pointer alive, a retention the handle already imposes (H5 in the design). A global registry of Systems would need a lifecycle of its own.
- A terminated owning System: non-guardian entries are gone from `byID`; guardians stay with `closed` set (`hierarchy.go:250-256`, `stop.go:109-110`), so a watch finds nothing or a closed guardian and delivers `Terminated`. Read, not run (design section 3.1).
- The retention statement is by holder and duration (design table H1-H5). The text does not say that nothing is retained. The progress of the removal rests on executor assumptions E1-E10 of the design; the demonstration is by reading (class READ), not executed and not implemented, and it found one placement change (foreign removals last in `acceptStop`) that the text above already assumes, and one residual (the removal would be skipped if `emit` or `Execute(c.drain)` panics, or ends the goroutine with Goexit under an inline executor), closed in the design by the deferred hand-off (Q5 direction accepted by the Owner; design document, 3.1 and section 7; not implemented).
- The proposed sentence "the removal is handed to the executor of the subject's System as the last step of accepting the stop" stays true on every path with the deferred hand-off registered right after the `c.mu` section of `acceptStop`: the normal path, a panic (in a hook or in the event sink) and a `Goexit` under an inline executor. Without the `defer` it would not hold under an inline executor, where a `Goexit` or a panic would skip the hand-off. The `defer` is a design direction: not implemented, its tests are defined (design 6.1 items 13-17) and not written. The proposed ADR text above is unchanged.

## Left as written

Line 162 stays as the record of 2026-10-10. Line 175 (locks) keeps its text; the design must preserve it (the lock caveats are in the design document, the [lock analysis](cross-system-watch-lock-analysis.md) and the [carrier B demonstration](cross-system-watch-carrier-b-demonstration.md), which also states the residual of the placement, closed in the design by the deferred hand-off: the foreign removal would be skipped if `emit` or `Execute(c.drain)` panics, or ends the goroutine with Goexit under an inline executor). `System.Stop` and `Context.Stop` treat a foreign handle as a no-op because its ID is absent from the caller's registry, not because of an owner check (`pekko-actor/actor/stop.go:20-24, 31-34`); `Done` answers `ErrUnknownHandle` (`pekko-actor/actor/system.go:183-193`); `pekko-actor/actor/behavior_test.go:145-162` must keep passing.
